Here are the requirements to make your custom browser trusted by Google:

1. Identify with a Valid User-Agent String

Google uses the User-Agent header to identify the browser. If your string is empty, generic, or mentions disallowed frameworks, Google will block it with a "403 Disallowed_Useragent" error.
• Format: Your browser's User-Agent must closely mimic a standard browser (like Chrome or Safari).
• Avoid blocked keywords: Do not include terms like WebView, Embed, or framework-specific names (like Electron or Qt) in the main user-agent string if they are known to trigger automated blocks.

2. Implement a Secure Rendering Engine

Google requires the browser to support modern web standards and isolation protocols.
• Use an approved engine: Build your browser on top of a trusted, up-to-date engine like Chromium (Blink) or Firefox (Gecko/GeckoView).
• Avoid raw WebViews: Standard mobile webviews (like Android WebView or iOS UIWebView) are completely blocked from Google OAuth sign-ins due to vulnerability to "man-in-the-middle" (MITM) attacks.

3. Handle Cookies and Javascript Perfectly

• Enable Full JavaScript: Google’s login page uses complex fingerprinting scripts to detect automated bots. Your engine must execute JavaScript exactly like Chrome or Firefox does.
• Support Cookies and Partitioning: The browser must properly store, send, and partition cookies (SameSite attributes) and support storage mechanisms like localStorage and IndexedDB.

4. Support Secure Connections (TLS/HTTPS)

• Modern Cipher Suites: Your browser must use secure, modern TLS configurations (TLS 1.2 or TLS 1.3).
• No Certificate Tampering: If your custom browser intercepts or modifies SSL/TLS certificates, Google's strict HTTP Strict Transport Security (HSTS) and certificate pinning will immediately block the connection.

5. Pass Bot Detection Checks (WebDriver / Automation Flags)

Google checks if the browser is an automated script (like Selenium or Puppeteer).
• Ensure standard automation flags (like window.navigator.webdriver) are false or not present during the login flow, as Google automatically blocks automated browsers from logging into personal accounts.
